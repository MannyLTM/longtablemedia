Is Halloween the Devil's Holy Day? What I Found After the Camera Stopped

An exorcist priest says Halloween is the devil's day. Here's where I landed: light beats darkness, but don't dance with the devil.

By Manny · October 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Hey! Every October the same debate comes up in the church: should Christians celebrate Halloween? Trunk-or-treat? Costumes? Skip it completely? So when an exorcist priest went on a podcast and called Halloween "the devil's holy day," I had to react. After the camera stopped, here's where I landed.

The devil is real, and he's not hiding anymore

The priest made a point I agree with: for years the enemy's biggest trick was convincing people he didn't exist. I've even talked to Christians who say they believe in Jesus but not in a real devil. But Jesus didn't go into the wilderness to fight an idea. And today the darkness isn't even hiding. It's on stage at halftime shows and award ceremonies, and even in a fast-food Halloween ad with a Ouija board.

Stay alert! Watch out for your great enemy, the devil. He prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour.1 Peter 5:8 (NLT)

A heavy claim, held carefully

The priest also shared a story he heard about people being kidnapped and sacrificed on Halloween. That's a serious claim, and I can't verify it, so I'm not going to repeat it as fact. What I can say is that some people really do treat this season as a time to go deeper into darkness, and we should be watchful.

So should Christians celebrate it?

Here's my honest take. If your town threw a costume party on August 20th with candy for the kids, nobody would call it evil. The problem isn't dressing up. The problem is what you're celebrating. Dressing up as a demon, playing with Ouija boards, or treating the night as a spiritual game? That's dancing with the devil, and if you dance with him, he's going to step on your toes.

Take no part in the worthless deeds of evil and darkness; instead, expose them.Ephesians 5:11 (NLT)

Light beats darkness

But I also see Halloween as one of the biggest outreach opportunities of the year. Your whole neighborhood comes knocking on your door. Hand out candy with a Bible verse. Smile. Pray for the families who walk by. It might be the darkest night of the year, but light beats darkness every single time.

So humble yourselves before God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.James 4:7 (NLT)
My conclusion: Don't celebrate darkness, and don't play with it. But don't hide from your neighbors either. As the priest put it, you plus Jesus always equals victory, so bring the light.
