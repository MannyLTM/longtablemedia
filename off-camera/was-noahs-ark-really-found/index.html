Was Noah's Ark Really Found? Here's What I Found After the Camera Stopped

Headlines said "100% confirmed." I reacted to it twice. Here's what's actually been found, and why my faith doesn't hang on a boat.

By Manny · October 3, 2026 · 4 min read

Hey! I've done two videos on this now: "Did They Really Find Noah's Ark?" and the one above, where a headline said Noah's Ark was "100% confirmed." I called it clickbait on camera. After I stopped recording, I wanted to make sure I wasn't just being a skeptic, so I checked where things actually stand.

What's actually been found (so far)

  • The site: a boat-shaped formation called Durupinar, in eastern Turkey near Mount Ararat. People have been looking at it since a pilot photographed it in 1959.
  • The new work: a team with a Turkish university and government permits ran new radar scans, then started core drilling in September 2026.
  • What they hit: a hard layer a few meters down that broke a drill bit. The team thinks it could be petrified wood. That's a possibility, not a result.
  • When we'll know more: lab results on the samples aren't expected until 2027.
  • The history: a Christian team drilled the same spot back in 1988 and reported hitting layered bedrock, not wood. Today's team says their tech goes much deeper, so they want a fresh look.

So "100% confirmed"? Not even close. It's "interesting, and we're testing." That's very different, and it's okay to say so.

The first to speak in court sounds right—until the cross-examination begins.Proverbs 18:17 (NLT)

That's every big headline. Wait for the cross-examination.

What the Bible actually says about the location

Exactly five months from the time the flood began, the boat came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.Genesis 8:4 (NLT)

Notice it says the mountains of Ararat, plural. That's a region (ancient Urartu), not one specific peak. So even the Bible doesn't hand us a GPS pin. That surprised me.

Here's the part that matters most

In both videos I said the same thing: even if they find it, people won't just start believing. Turns out Jesus said that first. In His story about the rich man and Lazarus, the rich man begs for a miracle so his brothers will believe. Here's the answer he gets:

If they won't listen to Moses and the prophets, they won't be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead.Luke 16:31 (NLT)

If a resurrection won't convince a hard heart, a boat won't either. And Noah himself didn't need proof. He had God's word:

It was by faith that Noah built a large boat to save his family from the flood. He obeyed God, who warned him about things that had never happened before.Hebrews 11:7 (NLT)
My conclusion: Nothing has been confirmed yet, and the "100%" headlines are clickbait. I'll be watching the 2027 lab results with you. But my faith doesn't rise or fall on a rock formation in Turkey. If they find the ark, amazing. If they don't, the flood still happened and God's word is still true. And Jesus said His return will be "like it was in Noah's day" (Matthew 24:37, NLT). That's the part of the story I don't want to miss.
