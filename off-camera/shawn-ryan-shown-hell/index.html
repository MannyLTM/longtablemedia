Shawn Ryan Says He Was Shown Hell. Here's What I Found After the Camera Stopped

Shawn Ryan told Cardinal Burke he believes he was shown hell during a psychedelic experience. Was it real, or was it the drugs? Here's how I'm thinking about it.

By Manny · October 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Hey! In this video I reacted to the opening of Shawn Ryan's Bible series with Cardinal Raymond Burke. Before they even got to the Old Testament, Shawn opened up about something personal: about a month earlier, during a psychedelic experience, he believes he was shown hell. He said there was no sense of God, nothing good, just extreme fear. After that came four exorcisms with two different priests, picture frames coming off the wall, and every smoke alarm in his house going off in the middle of the night.

That's a lot. And my big question was simple: was it real, or was it the drugs? So after the camera stopped, I went to the Bible.

Step one: test it

The Bible doesn't tell us to believe every spiritual experience. It tells us to check it.

Dear friends, do not believe everyone who claims to speak by the Spirit. You must test them to see if the spirit they have comes from God. For there are many false prophets in the world.1 John 4:1 (NLT)

And Paul warns that not everything that looks spiritual is from God:

But I am not surprised! Even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light.2 Corinthians 11:14 (NLT)

So I don't think we should build anything on a vision alone, good or bad. We measure it against Scripture.

About the psychedelics

Here's something I learned. In Galatians 5:20, Paul lists "sorcery" among the works of the sinful nature. The Greek word there is pharmakeia, which is connected to drugs and potions used in pagan spiritual practices. That's where we get the word "pharmacy."

I'm not saying every medicine is evil. But using a drug to open your mind to the spiritual world is opening a door God never told us to open. And honestly, Shawn's story kind of proves it: he went in looking for healing and came out face to face with something dark.

But what he saw lines up with Scripture

Here's what surprised me. Shawn described hell as empty, with no sense of God at all. Cardinal Burke said the same thing: there's nothing good there. That actually matches how the Bible describes it:

They will be punished with eternal destruction, forever separated from the Lord and from his glorious power.2 Thessalonians 1:9 (NLT)

The worst part of hell isn't the fire. It's that God isn't there. So whatever Shawn experienced, the picture he came back with is a biblical one.

Do we have to live in fear?

No. Four exorcisms is a scary thing to hear. But as a Christian, I don't need a ritual or a title to stand against the enemy. I need Jesus.

But you belong to God, my dear children. You have already won a victory over those people, because the Spirit who lives in you is greater than the spirit who lives in the world.1 John 4:4 (NLT)

I agreed with Cardinal Burke on one thing for sure: the devil's favorite weapon is discouragement. He wants you to quit. The answer is to keep going, stay in the Word, stay close to Jesus.

The part that encouraged me

Shawn said that as horrible as it was, he's thankful for it, because it strengthened his faith in Christ. That's God turning something dark into something good:

And we know that God causes everything to work together for the good of those who love God and are called according to his purpose for them.Romans 8:28 (NLT)
My conclusion: I can't tell you for sure what Shawn saw. A vision on psychedelics isn't something I'd trust on its own, and the Bible tells us to test every spirit. But what he described, a hell that's empty because God isn't there, matches Scripture. And where it led him, closer to Jesus, is the best possible ending. Don't go looking for the spiritual world through a drug. Go through the One who already beat it.
